## New
- Dungeon generator: Generate Dungeon in the asset manager's Add menu makes a ready scene with walls, doors, grid and lighting, on up to four floors joined by stairs
- Generated dungeons have a boss arena, cover, hazards, bridges over chasms, galleries and shafts; the Five-room switch builds the classic five-room dungeon
- Each generated dungeon gets a note with a heading per room and a GM-only numbered pin in each room (A1, B1 … on several floors)
- Sixteen looks for generated dungeons, drawn as stone and shadow or as an engraving, on vellum
- The note pin search offers to create a note of the name you typed, or to generate a dungeon of that name and pin it: the pin's panel turns into a short wizard (theme, layout, features, style, name) with the map drawn live beside it
- A random table row that links its own table rolls that table again without itself, for rows such as "Mithral + reroll"
- Generators: a note table whose rows are parts rolls them into one result, with a reroll and a lock per part, Give for results with a Type and Save as note; a link to a generator rolls all of it
- Scenes with several floors: add, name and stack them in the command palette's Scene levels page, and switch floors at the map's left edge or with Page Up and Page Down
- Each floor has its own map, tokens, walls, lights, fog and explored areas, and a floor's map is fitted to the scene's grid when you align it there
- Show players a floor with the eye in the floor switcher, and move tokens to another floor from their right-click menu
- Stairs between floors: a token put down on them goes to the floor they lead to, and an elevator lets you pick the floor
- Openings: draw a hole in a floor from the lighting menu to see the floor below through it; a token put down over it falls through
- Transparent parts of a floor's map show the floors below, two deep
- Roofs: give a floor a roof image, and it fades while a player character stands under it
- Import a building's Universal VTT files together as one scene with a floor each
- A pin that links a scene of several floors can open it on the floor you pick
- Following the turn shows the floor the token stands on
- Animated maps: WebM and MP4 videos play as maps, on the map and in the player window; Animate maps in Atlas' settings shows their first frame instead
- Replace map image: put a video, a gridless version or a larger export of the same map in its place, keeping the grid, walls, lights and tokens
- Map features: hover rubble, a statue or a door to see its name and traits, shown only to you; right-click a feature to rename it or set its terrain
- Difficult terrain costs double in a token's movement ruler where the game system has that rule (5e, Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel), with a switch in the collection's grid settings
- Cavern dungeons are natural caves: chambers and tunnels in the rock with a way two squares wide to the boss, chasms along the faults, lakes in the low chambers, pitches and scree slopes between floors, and lava or acid deep down where the tags ask for them
- With the Inhabited tag a cavern near water is lived in: fire pits in its hollows and at a guard post by its mouth, and the chief's den at the end
- Generated dungeons show structure, not furniture: statues, busts, fountains, wells, daises and fireplaces, and in caves stalagmites, crystals, mushrooms and boulders, so what each room is for is yours to decide
- Encounter builder: type dice such as 2d4 into a creature's count, and every time the encounter is placed that many are rolled
- Note pins can link to a folder of encounters: a click on the pin rolls one of the encounters in it (folders below it too) and puts it in your hand to place; Cmd/Ctrl + hover lists them to pick one yourself

## Improved

- On Balanced and Saver lighting quality, a high-resolution screen draws the lit map at standard resolution, which takes the graphics less work; the edges of what players see never reach further
- A device without a lighting quality chosen in the settings starts on one picked for its graphics: Balanced on graphics built into the processor, Saver where WebGL draws on the CPU
- Acid on generated maps moves gently in the artwork’s own pigments, with slow bubbles and ink outlines in engraved looks.
- Lava moves as slowly drifting crust and molten channels, with hatching on engraved maps and matching tones on Monochrome maps
- Water on generated dungeon maps moves in the drawing’s own style: soft painted reflections or quiet engraved ripples, with gentler banks and cheaper shading at lower graphics quality.
- The update notice comes back each time Obsidian starts until you click it, instead of showing only once

## Important changes

- A scene with several floors is saved in a newer format that older versions of Atlas cannot open; scenes with one floor are saved as before
